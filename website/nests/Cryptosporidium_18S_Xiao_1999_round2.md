## Scope

This protocol's scope is the intersection of its rounds' scopes: a record counts only if every round claims it, so a record one round's target covers and another's does not is out of scope rather than a failure.

**Meant to detect:** Cryptosporidium, 18S rRNA gene, as the nested second round, with digestion to identify species.  
**Scored against:** only taxa that every round claims.  
**Retrieved from:** Cryptosporidium (taxid 5806), limited by a query on the title or gene.

The protocol declares no scope restriction, so every record retrieved for its target taxid counts toward the denominator. Where an assay was designed for a narrower range than its taxid covers, that presents as low coverage rather than drift; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

Retrieval query:

```
18S[Title] OR "small subunit ribosomal"[Title] OR SSU[Title]
```

A retrieval query is a claim about which records the assay is tested against, and it can stop being accurate without producing an error; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

## What was deposited, by lineage

Lineages here are sequence groups, not species or taxids. Each is a group of records
that look alike over the amplicon and its flanks with the oligo sites masked, cut at
0.05 Mash distance. Records can link one species to the next, so a group
can hold several; a percent in its name is the share of its top species. The groups
come from sequence this assay does not measure, so the mix below can move without the
assay changing. The dashed line on the chart above recomputes each year on the whole
period's mix: where the two lines part, what got sequenced changed, not what the assay
detects.

**The largest group here, C. parvum 23%, holds several species names,** which may be
different species or one species under more than one name. The dashed line can't separate
a shift between species within a group. The table below shows the names in each group.
