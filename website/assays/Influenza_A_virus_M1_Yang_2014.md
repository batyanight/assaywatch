## Scope

**Meant to detect:** Influenza A virus matrix (M) gene, degenerate version of an earlier test, for circulating human H1N1pdm09 and H3N2.  
**Scored against:** every record retrieved.  
**Retrieved from:** Influenza A virus (taxid 11320), limited by a query on the title or gene.

This entry declares no scope restriction, so every record retrieved for its target taxid counts toward the denominator. Where an assay was designed for a narrower range than its taxid covers, that presents as low coverage rather than drift; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

Retrieval query:

```
"segment 7"[Title] OR ("matrix protein"[Title] AND M[Gene])
```

A retrieval query is a claim about which records the assay is tested against, and it can stop being accurate without producing an error; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

## Records reaching the statistics

185,845 records were screened for this assay and **184,860 (99%)** were evaluable, meaning both primers landed in the right orientation and size range. 985 produced no amplicon at all.

This matters because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

## What was deposited, by lineage

Lineages here are sequence groups, not species or taxids. Each is a group of records
that look alike over the amplicon and its flanks with the oligo sites masked, cut at
0.05 Mash distance. Records can link one species to the next, so a group
can hold several; a percent in its name is the share of its top species. The groups
come from sequence this assay does not measure, so the mix below can move without the
assay changing. The dashed line on the chart above recomputes each year on the whole
period's mix: where the two lines part, what got sequenced changed, not what the assay
detects.

Tested against a real record (PZ777374.2) with the project's own matcher, that sequence binds the PLUS strand; its reverse complement binds the MINUS strand at exactly the site Ward's antisenseA occupies (216-238), which is where a working reverse primer sits. The stored form is therefore the functional primer. printed.py's own verifier reads the stored sequence as `revcomp` against this PDF and says so in as many words -- "listed as its reverse complement, which is normal for a reverse primer" -- so the project already treats a paper printing the template-strand form as ordinary. It is recorded because the choice is not cosmetic: the scorer's 3' window carries a penalty of 3.0 against 0.5 at the 5' end, so storing the mirrored form would mis-price every mismatch in the primer, and on a drift case that is the whole measurement. modified_from holds Ward's primer in the same (functional) orientation, so the two entries are directly comparable.</p><p><b>2026-10-30</b> OLIGOS VERIFIED AGAINST THE HELD PDF 2026-10-30. curate could not fetch this paper (PMC3911422 carries no full text and neither the Unpaywall nor the OpenAlex link served a PDF), so its oligo step was skipped and the check was run directly against papers/yang-2014-10.1128_JCM.02467-13.pdf with printed.compare: forward `exact`, probe `exact`, reverse `revcomp` as described above.
