## Scope

**Meant to detect:** Orthobunyaviruses of the California encephalitis and Bunyamwera serogroups, S segment.  
**Scored against:** every record retrieved.  
**Retrieved from:** Orthobunyavirus (taxid 11572), limited by a query on the title or gene.

This entry declares no scope restriction, so every record retrieved for its target taxid counts toward the denominator. Where an assay was designed for a narrower range than its taxid covers, that presents as low coverage rather than drift; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

Retrieval query:

```
S[Gene] OR M[Gene] OR "S segment"[Title] OR "M segment"[Title] OR nucleocapsid[Title] OR glycoprotein[Title] OR "complete genome"[Title]
```

A retrieval query is a claim about which records the assay is tested against, and it can stop being accurate without producing an error; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

## Records reaching the statistics

This matters because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

## What was deposited, by lineage

Lineages here are sequence groups, not species or taxids. Each is a group of records
that look alike over the amplicon and its flanks with the oligo sites masked, cut at
0.05 Mash distance. Records can link one species to the next, so a group
can hold several; a percent in its name is the share of its top species. The groups
come from sequence this assay does not measure, so the mix below can move without the
assay changing. The dashed line on the chart above recomputes each year on the whole
period's mix: where the two lines part, what got sequenced changed, not what the assay
detects.

Single round. The panel notes the source paper gives primer sets for all three segments and recommends screening with at least two; the M-segment assay is P-023. All three terminal primers (BUNS-5-U-6, BUNS-3-L947, BUN-S3-R) carry a non-templated 5' cloning tail; while they were entered untailed, 0 of 2056 orthobunyavirus records yielded an amplicon. Evidence that first identified the tails (2026-10-04): the conserved terminus is present in only 4% of deposits, and even in records that carry it the full primer finds no site within 6 mismatches, while dropping CGGCGCC from BUNS-5-U-6 matches perfectly (0 mismatches in 6 of 6 records) and dropping GCGGCC from BUNS-3-L947 leaves 1. GGCGCC is a KasI/NarI site. Tails CONFIRMED 2026-10-04 against Briese et al. 2007 Table 1 (doi:10.1007/s00705-007-1069-z), obtained in full. The table's footnote reads "Non-authentic bases added to some primers are indicated by italics", and the italics are carried by a distinct font face in the PDF, so the boundary is readable rather than inferred: CGGCGCC on BUNS-5-U-6, GCGGCC on BUNS-3-L947, CGCGCC on BUN-S3-R, and no italic run on M3560-F or on any of the paper's internal primers. All three boundaries match what was inferred from sequence data on 2026-10-04 character for character, which is the one independent check this registry has had of the tail-inference method. tail_5prime is now set. Note BUN-S3-R's authentic part is 14 nt, one below the registry's oligo floor; the floor for a declared tail's binding part was lowered to 14 rather than the published primer being rewritten. AMPLICON: declared 930 bp replaced by amplicon_bp_range [283, 1120] on 2026-10-21. The 930 is one orthobunyavirus' segment length (Briese 2007's reference virus); this is a pan-genus consensus assay and the segment length is a species continuum, so a scalar clipped most of it. MEASURED by rescreening the 11572 archives with the size window opened: 59 amplifying (detect/reduced) records span 293-1110 bp across ~26 species, modal 754.
