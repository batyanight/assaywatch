## Mismatch distribution

Each oligonucleotide drawn base by base, 5′ to 3′. The shaded region is the one the
scoring penalizes more heavily, and it differs by oligonucleotide: for a primer the
3′ window, where a mismatch sits in the polymerase’s catalytic site; for a probe the
central third, since a hydrolysis probe is never extended — its 3′ end carries the
quencher and it is cleaved by exonuclease activity, so what matters is duplex
stability. Bar height is the proportion of records differing at that position; bar
color is the consequence, measured as the fall in detection between records
differing there and those not:

<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-strong"></span> detection falls by 25 points or more</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-mid"></span> falls 10 to 25</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-weak"></span> little or no effect — variation the assay tolerates</span>

Counts are available on hover. A tall gray bar indicates a tolerated polymorphism; a
short red bar indicates a position of consequence.

healthy while one clade fails completely. Worst first. Lineages with too few records

## Scope

This entry declares no scope restriction, so every record retrieved for its target taxid counts toward the denominator. Where an assay was designed for a narrower range than its taxid covers, that presents as low coverage rather than drift; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

## Records reaching the statistics

16,099 records were screened for this assay and **4,292 (27%)** were evaluable, meaning both primers landed in the right orientation and size range. 11,807 produced no amplicon at all.

This is material because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

ANCESTOR FOUND 2026-10-22: Aitichou et al. 2005, doi:10.1016/j.jviromet.2004.10.004, J Virol Methods 124:21-26, is the existing protocol Anthony modified - it is Anthony reference 56, and Anthony states the hantavirus consensus primers were modified from it to increase the degeneracy of the assay. Aitichou Table 1 prints HANTAV1U (GWGGVCARACAGCWGAYT, positions 374-391) and HANTAV1L (TCCWGGTGTAADYTCHTCWGC, 604-624) against the S segments of Hantaan NC_005218, Dobrava NC_005233, Puumala NC_005224 and Seoul NC_005236. UHantaF1 is HANTAV1U with two positions widened (A to W, W to H); UHantaR1 is HANTAV1L minus its leading T and with A to D at one position. So the line is Aitichou 2005 designed it, Anthony 2013 amended and renamed it, and the amended pair is attributed to Anthony because UHantaF1 and UHantaR1 are the names Anthony prints - a primer is named for the paper it comes from. Aitichou is recorded as ancestry only and deliberately not as the citation, because it does not print these sequences: the same rule that kept Coiras 2004 off the Enterovirus pair. Held as papers/10.1016_j.jviromet.2004.10.004.pdf. NEXT for the unattributed rounds: Anthony supplemental material is Text S1 (DOCX), Figure S1, Figure S2 and Table S1 (PDF), and Table S1 is the herpesvirus cooccurrence table, so Text S1 is the only remaining candidate for UHantaF2/R2, UHantaF3/R3 and UHantaF4.
