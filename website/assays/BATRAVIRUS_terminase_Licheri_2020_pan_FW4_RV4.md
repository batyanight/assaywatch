## Mismatch distribution

Each oligonucleotide drawn base by base, 5′ to 3′. The shaded region is the one the
scoring penalizes more heavily, and it differs by oligonucleotide: for a primer the
3′ window, where a mismatch sits in the polymerase’s catalytic site; for a probe the
central third, since a hydrolysis probe is never extended — its 3′ end carries the
quencher and it is cleaved by exonuclease activity, so what matters is duplex
stability. Bar height is the proportion of records differing at that position; bar
color is the consequence, measured as the fall in detection between records
differing there and those not:

<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-strong"></span> detection falls by 25 points or more</span>

<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-weak"></span> little or no effect — variation the assay tolerates</span>

Counts are available on hover. A tall gray bar indicates a tolerated polymorphism; a
short red bar indicates a position of consequence.

Target: a 200-bp region of the DNA packaging terminase gene (ORF53 in RaHV-3, ORF39 in BfHV-1 per the paper). MEASURED on every Batravirus record in GenBank (txid3044671, which NCBI now names Batravirus, formerly Batrachovirus; retrieved 2026-10-06, 17 records, all of them): the four complete genomes the paper names (DQ665917 RaHV-1, DQ665652 RaHV-2, KX832224 RaHV-3, MF143550 BfHV-1) plus their four RefSeq copies (NC_008211, NC_008210, NC_034618, NC_040681) = 8 spanning records for 4 distinct viruses. The amplicon is 200 bp in all four, exactly the paper's 200, and the oligos sit where the paper says: RaHV-3 forward 70,708-70,733 and reverse 70,888-70,907; BfHV-1 50,454-50,653 (that record is deposited in the opposite orientation to the others, so the forward primer matches its minus strand); RaHV-1 153,618-153,817; RaHV-2 162,581-162,780. The other 9 records (MG800826, MT975973, PV005823-PV005829; 90-1550 bp, the PV records flagged UNVERIFIED) are short fragments of other loci (the PV records map by exact 20-mer match to ~65.8 kb of MF143550, MG800826 to ~99.0 kb of KX832224, and MT975973 is titled a polymerase gene fragment; the terminase amplicon is at ~50.5 kb in MF143550 and ~70.7 kb in KX832224) and none contains the amplicon; their no_amplicon calls are coverage, not primer failure, and are excluded from the detected share. Typos in the paper's own coordinates, for the record: the RaHV-1 reverse primer is printed as 153,617-153,598 but is at 153,798-153,817 (the paper gives the amplicon as 153,618-153,817 elsewhere); SN-RV-1 on BfHV-1 is printed 50,557-50,536 but measured 50,550-50,571 (the RaHV-3 position, 70,790-70,811, is right). Neither affects these two entries. SCOPE: both protocols are single-locus consensus assays on the genus; the dsDNA terminase is the only amplicon.</p><p>THIS ENTRY: EUPB-FW-4 (26 nt) + EUPB-RV-4 (20 nt), both heavily degenerate (FW-4 differs from FW-1 at 7 positions and RV-4 from RV-1 at 5, counted from Table 1), the paper's panbatrachovirus protocol. RESULT: RaHV-3 detect (penalties 0, 0), BfHV-1 detect (0, 0), RaHV-1 'reduced' (FW-4 2 mismatches, RV-4 1; penalties 1 and 1), RaHV-2 'reduced' (FW-4 3 mismatches, RV-4 1; penalties 1.5 and 1): 4 of 4 distinct viruses give a 200-bp product, 2 detect and 2 reduced. That matches the paper's ordering: LOD 10^3 copies for RaHV-1, RaHV-3 and BfHV-1 and 10^4 for RaHV-2, i.e. it amplifies all four but is less sensitive than the European pair on RaHV-3 and BfHV-1. The scope here is the whole genus, which is what the paper designed it for. Only four batrachovirus genomes exist in GenBank, so 'all known batrachoviruses' is four sequences; the screen is a mismatch count against those four, not a coverage estimate.</p></details>
