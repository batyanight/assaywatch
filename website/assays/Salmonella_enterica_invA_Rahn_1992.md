## Scope

**Meant to detect:** Salmonella enterica (all serovars), invA gene.  
**Scored against:** every record retrieved.  
**Retrieved from:** Salmonella enterica (taxid 28901), limited by a query on the title or gene.

This entry declares no scope restriction, so every record retrieved for its target taxid counts toward the denominator. Where an assay was designed for a narrower range than its taxid covers, that presents as low coverage rather than drift; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

Retrieval query:

```
(invA[Gene] OR "invasion protein"[Title] OR invA[Title]) AND 1:30000[SLEN] NOT "whole genome shotgun"[Title]
```

A retrieval query is a claim about which records the assay is tested against, and it can stop being accurate without producing an error; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

Disclosures on this entry: `amplicon_disagrees_with_paper`.

## Records reaching the statistics

1,003 records were screened for this assay and **464 (46%)** were evaluable, meaning both primers landed in the right orientation and size range. 539 produced no amplicon at all.

This matters because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

## What was deposited, by lineage

Lineages here are sequence groups, not species or taxids. Each is a group of records
that look alike over the amplicon and its flanks with the oligo sites masked, cut at
0.05 Mash distance. Records can link one species to the next, so a group
can hold several; a percent in its name is the share of its top species. The groups
come from sequence this assay does not measure, so the mix below can move without the
assay changing. The dashed line on the chart above recomputes each year on the whole
period's mix: where the two lines part, what got sequenced changed, not what the assay
detects.

<details class="aw-curation"><summary>The full note, as the curator wrote it · in the order the curator wrote it, oldest first</summary><p><b>2026-10-20</b> OLIGOS ARE NOT RAHN'S PUBLISHED PAIR. Read 2026-10-20 from the local PDF, which has a text layer. Rahn's Table 3 prints exactly two primers, both in lower case: 139 gtgaaattatcgccacgttcgggcaa at invA 287-312, and 141 tcatcgcaccgtcaaaggaacc at 571-550. This entry's Sal-3 is the 3'-terminal 19 nt of 139, missing GTGAAAT from the 5' end; Sal-4 is the 3'-terminal 19 nt of 141, missing TCA. The 3' ends are identical, so the binding sites are the same and the truncation can only lower the penalty an oligo can accumulate, which makes this entry a MORE permissive assay than the paper's. The sizes agree with that reading rather than with the paper: Table 3's locations give 285 bp and the text says "the specific 284 bp product", while 285 minus the 7 and 3 dropped bases is 275, and the 2026-10-17 calls measure 275 bp in 467 of 468 detected or reduced records. So amplicon_bp: 275 is correct FOR THE OLIGOS AS STORED and wrong for the citation. Not changed here: substituting 139/141 changes every call for this assay and is a maintainer's decision, the same way the Chlamydia genus 16S primer was kept as printed on 2026-10-17. Note that the validation case backing this entry (Malorny 2003, 99.6 percent inclusivity on 364 strains) validated 139/141, not this pair. Why it was missed until now: the paper has been on disk all along, but scripts/verify_oligos_against_paper.py reported no-full-text, "no local PDF; needs the PDF". Two independent reasons, both in that script. (1) The papers/ naming convention maps /, ( and ) all to _, and the reverse mapping only undoes /, so this file's stem reconstructs as 10.1016/0890-8508/92/90002-f, which still fullmatches the DOI pattern and so indexes the paper under a DOI nothing asks for while also marking it "named" and barring it from the surname-and-year fallback. Ten PDFs on disk, covering 20 registry entries, have parenthesised DOIs and are unreachable this way, Meyer 1997 ATI and Lozano 1997 among them. (2) printed_oligos requires at least 60 percent upper case, on the stated premise that "papers set sequences in capitals"; Rahn sets both primers entirely in lower case, so it extracts 0 oligos from this text even though both sequences are present in it.
