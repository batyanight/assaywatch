## Mismatch distribution

Each oligonucleotide drawn base by base, 5′ to 3′. The shaded region is the one the
scoring penalizes more heavily, and it differs by oligonucleotide: for a primer the
3′ window, where a mismatch sits in the polymerase’s catalytic site; for a probe the
central third, since a hydrolysis probe is never extended — its 3′ end carries the
quencher and it is cleaved by exonuclease activity, so what matters is duplex
stability. Bar height is the proportion of records differing at that position; bar
color is the consequence, measured as the fall in detection between records
differing there and those not:

<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-strong"></span> detection falls by 25 points or more</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-mid"></span> falls 10 to 25</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-weak"></span> little or no effect — variation the assay tolerates</span>

Counts are available on hover. A tall gray bar indicates a tolerated polymorphism; a
short red bar indicates a position of consequence.

Found by scripts/verify_oligos_against_paper.py as a near:1 hit. The table prints, with superscript positions in the ystA reading frame:\n yst-Pr2a 5'-(30)AATGCTGTCTTCATTTGGAGC(50)-3'\n yst-Pr2b 5'-(92)GCAACATACATCACAGCAATC(72)-3'\n yst-P probe CAAGCAAGCTTGTGATCCTC\nThe reverse primer was stored as GCAACATACATCGCAGCAATC - a G where the paper has an A, 9 bases from the 3' end. Corrected to the paper's base.\nWHAT THE CORRECTION ACTUALLY DID, which is more interesting than a typo. Screened against the 35 Y. enterocolitica assemblies both ways, the result inverts: with the stored G, 13 of 35 records match perfectly and 22 carry a mismatch; with the paper's A, 22 match perfectly and 13 carry one. So this position is genuinely polymorphic in the target - both bases circulate - and the paper's primer sits on the majority allele while the stored variant sat on the minority one. An R at that position would match all 35, but that would be designing a new primer rather than recording a published one, so it is noted and not done.\nThe error was SILENT to the drift metric: a single mismatch 9 bases from the 3' end costs exactly 1.0, and detect_max is 1.0, so all 35 records read `detect` either way. The tool cannot catch its own input errors; only checking against the paper can. It was not harmless, though - it spent the entire detect budget, so any second mismatch anywhere would have tipped a record to `reduced` for a reason that did not exist.\nTWO DISCREPANCIES REMAIN UNRESOLVED and are deliberately not 'fixed', because unlike the base above they cannot be settled by reading the table:\n 1. The forward primer. Stored YE-1 is CTGTCTTCATTTGGAGCATTC; the paper's yst-Pr2a is AATGCTGTCTTCATTTGGAGC. They share a 17 nt core but the stored one is shifted 4 bases 3'-ward, losing AATG from the 5' end and gaining ATTC at the 3' end. A 3'-end change is exactly what matters most for extension, so this is not a cosmetic difference. It could be a deliberate redesign or a transcription slip, and rewriting it on a guess would be worse than flagging it.\n 2. amplicon_bp is 159, but the paper's positions give 92 - 30 + 1 = 63 bp. Nothing in the paper states 159. With the stored forward primer the span would be about 59 bp. Where 159 came from is unknown; the seed list is the likely origin.\nAlso recorded: the paper names these yst-Pr2a / yst-Pr2b, not YE-1 / YE-2, and the target is the ystA enterotoxin reading frame. YE-1/YE-2 are the seed list's names.</p></details>
