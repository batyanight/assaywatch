This entry declares no scope restriction, so every record retrieved for its target taxid counts toward the denominator. Where an assay was designed for a narrower range than its taxid covers, that presents as low coverage rather than drift; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

## Records reaching the statistics

2,018 records were screened for this assay and **84 (4%)** were evaluable, meaning both primers landed in the right orientation and size range. 1,934 produced no amplicon at all.

This matters because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

## What was deposited, by lineage

Lineages here are sequence groups, not species or taxids. Each is a group of records
that look alike over the amplicon and its flanks with the oligo sites masked, cut at
0.05 Mash distance. Records can link one species to the next, so a group
can hold several; a percent in its name is the share of its top species. The groups
come from sequence this assay does not measure, so the mix below can move without the
assay changing. The dashed line on the chart above recomputes each year on the whole
period's mix: where the two lines part, what got sequenced changed, not what the assay
detects.

*5 of 84 diverged, with no site to cluster on.*

<details class="aw-curation"><summary>The full note, as the curator wrote it · in the order the curator wrote it, oldest first</summary><p><b>2026-10-04</b> Added 2026-10-04 from Briese et al. 2007 Table 1 (doi:10.1007/s00705-007-1069-z), read from the paper itself. The table gives each primer's sequence and the genome position of its 3'-terminal base, numbered on the Bunyamwera virus prototype 1943 reference (D00353 = S, M11852 = M, X14383 = L). Degenerate positions are printed as lower-case IUPAC codes and are transcribed here in upper case; sequences are printed in triplets, which is how each primer's length was checked. Non-authentic (cloning-tail) bases are marked in the table by a distinct italic font, and NONE of the primers in this entry carries one except where tail_5prime is set below. amplicon_bp is COMPUTED, not quoted: the paper gives 3' positions, not product sizes, so the product is taken as the span from the forward primer's 5' base to the reverse primer's 5' base using the printed 3' positions and the primer lengths. BUN-S5-F carries a 5 nt italic tail (GCCGC); its 20 nt of authentic sequence has its 3' base at 20, so it binds 1-20, the M-segment 5' terminus. M940C-R is 26 nt with its 3' base at 936, so it binds 936-961; the product is 1-961 = 961 bp. The arithmetic is corroborated by the primer names, which encode their own positions (BUNS-3-L947 at 947, BUNL650R at 601-630). Treat the size as approximate: it is exact only on the Bunyamwera reference, and these are consensus primers meant for a whole serogroup. SCOPE. The paper designed these from an alignment of 'orthobunyaviruses of the CE and BUN serogroups and Guaroa virus', so the claim is the California encephalitis and Bunyamwera serogroups, not the whole genus. The registry target is genus Orthobunyavirus (~18 serogroups), so a low detected share here is very likely scope rather than drift. Screening the two assays already in the registry on 2026-10-04 showed the pattern plainly: strong on the design serogroups (Cache Valley 14/15, Maguari 4/5) and near-zero on Simbu-serogroup viruses (Oropouche 0/24, Akabane 0/7, Schmallenberg 0/7, Manzanilla 0/4).
