## Mismatch distribution

Each oligonucleotide drawn base by base, 5′ to 3′. The shaded region is the one the
scoring penalizes more heavily, and it differs by oligonucleotide: for a primer the
3′ window, where a mismatch sits in the polymerase’s catalytic site; for a probe the
central third, since a hydrolysis probe is never extended — its 3′ end carries the
quencher and it is cleaved by exonuclease activity, so what matters is duplex
stability. Bar height is the proportion of records differing at that position; bar
color is the consequence, measured as the fall in detection between records
differing there and those not:

<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-strong"></span> detection falls by 25 points or more</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-mid"></span> falls 10 to 25</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-weak"></span> little or no effect — variation the assay tolerates</span>

Counts are available on hover. A tall gray bar indicates a tolerated polymorphism; a
short red bar indicates a position of consequence.

## Records reaching the statistics

12,691 records were screened for this assay and **457 (4%)** were evaluable, meaning both primers landed in the right orientation and size range. 12,234 produced no amplicon at all.

This is material because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

<details class="aw-curation"><summary>The full note, as the curator wrote it · in the order the curator wrote it, oldest first</summary><p><b>2026-10-05</b> Added 2026-10-05 from the paper's own text, read out of the PDF in papers/ rather than transcribed from any secondary list. Candidate surfaced by the journal sweep in docs/journal/2026-10-04-literature-audit.md; sequences extracted with scripts/extract_candidate_oligos.py and then checked against real GenBank records before the entry was trusted. White-nose syndrome was entirely absent from this registry, which was the single largest gap the sweep found among top-tier wildlife pathogens. VERIFIED: both primers and the probe were located in real P. destructans ITS records, giving a 140-141 bp product with the probe inside the amplicon (PP958286.1 at 140 bp, PX393942.1 at 141 bp). amplicon_bp is therefore measured, not quoted - the paper describes the ITS1 region as 171 bp but does not state a product size. Two of four sampled records did not yield a product; those are longer ITS submissions where the forward site falls outside the deposited span, which is the usual pattern for a short qPCR amplicon against variable-length rDNA submissions and not evidence against the assay.</p><p>THE SECOND PROBE IS DELIBERATELY NOT POOLED. The paper runs two probes on the same primer pair: FAM CTTGCCAGAGGACTAA, specific to P. destructans, which is the one recorded here, and VIC CTTGCCAGAGGACCTA, specific to other Pseudogymnoascus/Geomyces species - they differ by a single inserted C. Pooling them would make the entry report detection of the wrong organism, since the whole point of the pair is to tell the pathogen from its harmless relatives. If the congener probe is ever wanted it belongs in its own entry with its own target taxon. Both probes carry a non-fluorescent quencher and a minor groove binder, hence chemistry mgb; the 16 nt probe is only viable because of the MGB.</p></details>
