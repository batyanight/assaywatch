## Mismatch distribution

Each oligonucleotide drawn base by base, 5′ to 3′. The shaded region is the one the
scoring penalizes more heavily, and it differs by oligonucleotide: for a primer the
3′ window, where a mismatch sits in the polymerase’s catalytic site; for a probe the
central third, since a hydrolysis probe is never extended — its 3′ end carries the
quencher and it is cleaved by exonuclease activity, so what matters is duplex
stability. Bar height is the proportion of records differing at that position; bar
color is the consequence, measured as the fall in detection between records
differing there and those not:

<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-strong"></span> detection falls by 25 points or more</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-mid"></span> falls 10 to 25</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-weak"></span> little or no effect — variation the assay tolerates</span>

Counts are available on hover. A tall gray bar indicates a tolerated polymorphism; a
short red bar indicates a position of consequence.

healthy while one clade fails completely. Worst first. Lineages with too few records

## Records reaching the statistics

4,805 records were screened for this assay and **4,012 (83%)** were evaluable, meaning both primers landed in the right orientation and size range. 793 produced no amplicon at all.

This is material because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

Three further 'fail' calls (743, 1,383 and 451 bp: AH008262, AH008265, FO203510) are spurious long products from single-primer partial matches in IS6110 flank sequences, not the target. COVERAGE: of the 552 short records, 370 contain neither primer-site flank (IS6110 flank fragments, 23S rRNA and other loci of 200-600 bp, e.g. U24533, X95145) and 8 are 200-245 bp records that end inside the amplicon; none of the short records that contain both sites fails to amplify (no_amplicon with both flanks present: 0). GENOMES: 36 MTBC genome records (RefSeq NZ_ copies included): 33 detect, 3 give no product and carry neither primer site at all (AP024458 M. tuberculosis DN101, and M. canettii CIPT 140070010 and CIPT 140070017, which lack IS6110 copies; confirmed by exact string search for both primers). All 8 non-MTBC genomes in the set (6 M. kansasii records, 2 M. vaccae) give no product, so the pair does not cross-amplify those. IS6110 copy number varies from 1 (most M. bovis, BCG Pasteur 1173P2) to over a dozen (many M. tuberculosis), so the pipeline's detect share is a statement about presence of the element, and IS6110-less MTBC strains (some M. canettii, rare M. tuberculosis) are not detected by design.</p><p>NOT ENTERED FROM THIS PAPER: the second primer pair, IM_dir (5'-CTACTACGCTCAACGCCAG-3') and IM_rev (5'-ATCGACTGCGACAACATCC-3'), described as amplifying the DR region 3' of IS6110 to give 573 or 387 bp for sequencing. It is a genotyping/sequencing primer pair, not a detection assay, and it could not be reproduced on real records: IM_dir matches exactly inside every IS6110 copy and IM_rev matches exactly once, in a spacer at the end of the DR locus, in most MTBC genomes (checked in detail on the M. microti genome CP010333, where the IS6110 inserted in the DR locus has its IM_dir site and the IM_rev spacer both pointing towards lower coordinates, so the two primers cannot pair), but in CP010333 and in the 35 other MTBC genome records no IM_dir site and IM_rev site face each other within 3 kb (the best approximate pair in CP010333 is 844 bp with 5 reverse-primer mismatches), and no product near 387 or 573 bp exists in any genome or short record screened. It is held back as unresolved, not as wrong; the wild-boar isolates' own DR sequences carry no accession in the paper.
