central third, since a hydrolysis probe is never extended — its 3′ end carries the
quencher and it is cleaved by exonuclease activity, so what matters is duplex
stability. Bar height is the proportion of records differing at that position; bar

## Records reaching the statistics

386 records were screened for this assay and **28 (7%)** were evaluable, meaning both primers landed in the right orientation and size range. 358 produced no amplicon at all.

This is material because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

## Curation record

OLIGO SOURCE FOUND 2026-10-23, and it is not the cited paper. Both stored oligos read `absent` against Victor et al. 1991, which prints its own LT primers as LT51 CCCGTATTACAGAAATCTGA and LT31 GTGCATGATGAATCCAGGGT in Fig. 1 and names no oligo EcoLT. The stored pair is printed in Wang, R-F., Cao, W-W. and Cerniglia, C.E. 1997, J Appl Microbiol 83:727-736 (10.1046/j.1365-2672.1997.00300.x), whose Table 2 gives EcoLT-1 GAGACCGGTATTACAGAAATC and EcoLT-2 GAGGTGCATGATGAATCCAG with a 117 bp product - the names, both sequences and the amplicon this entry stores - and lists "Victor et al. (1991)" in its own Reference column, where its newly designed assays instead read "This study". The same pair, attributed to Wang 1997, appears in Table 1 of 10.1371/journal.pone.0101456 (PMC4077799). So the sequences are Wang's and the Victor attribution is one Wang made.
