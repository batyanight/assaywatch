## Mismatch distribution

Each oligo drawn base by base, 5′ to 3′. The shaded region is the one the scoring
penalizes more heavily, and it depends on the oligo. For a primer it is the 3′ window,
where a mismatch sits in the polymerase’s catalytic site. For a probe it is the central
third: a hydrolysis probe is never extended, its 3′ end carries the quencher and it is
cleaved by exonuclease activity, so what matters is duplex stability. Bar height is the
share of records that differ at that position. Bar color is what that costs, measured
as the fall in detection between records that differ there and records that do not:

<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-strong"></span> detection falls by 25 points or more</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-mid"></span> falls 10 to 25</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-weak"></span> little or no effect: variation the assay tolerates</span>

Hover for counts. A tall gray bar is a polymorphism the assay tolerates; a short red
bar is a position that matters.

The table prints, with superscript positions in the ystA reading frame:\n yst-Pr2a 5'-(30)AATGCTGTCTTCATTTGGAGC(50)-3'\n yst-Pr2b 5'-(92)GCAACATACATCACAGCAATC(72)-3'\n yst-P probe CAAGCAAGCTTGTGATCCTC\nThe reverse primer was stored as GCAACATACATCGCAGCAATC - a G where the paper has an A, 9 bases from the 3' end. Corrected to the paper's base.\nWHAT THE CORRECTION ACTUALLY DID, which is more interesting than a typo. Screened against the 35 Y. enterocolitica assemblies both ways, the result inverts: with the stored G, 13 of 35 records match perfectly and 22 carry a mismatch; with the paper's A, 22 match perfectly and 13 carry one. So this position is genuinely polymorphic in the target - both bases circulate - and the paper's primer sits on the majority allele while the stored variant sat on the minority one. An R at that position would match all 35, but that would be designing a new primer rather than recording a published one, so it is noted and not done.\nThe error was SILENT to the drift metric: a single mismatch 9 bases from the 3' end costs exactly 1.0, and detect_max is 1.0, so all 35 records read `detect` either way. The tool cannot catch its own input errors; only checking against the paper can. It was not harmless, though - it spent the entire detect budget, so any second mismatch anywhere would have tipped a record to `reduced` for a reason that did not exist.\nTWO DISCREPANCIES REMAIN UNRESOLVED and are deliberately not 'fixed', because unlike the base above they cannot be settled by reading the table:\n 1. The forward primer. Stored YE-1 is CTGTCTTCATTTGGAGCATTC; the paper's yst-Pr2a is AATGCTGTCTTCATTTGGAGC. They share a 17 nt core but the stored one is shifted 4 bases 3'-ward, losing AATG from the 5' end and gaining ATTC at the 3' end. A 3'-end change is exactly what matters most for extension, so this is not a cosmetic difference. It could be a deliberate redesign or a transcription slip, and rewriting it on a guess would be worse than flagging it.\n 2. amplicon_bp is 159, but the paper's positions give 92 - 30 + 1 = 63 bp. Nothing in the paper states 159. With the stored forward primer the span would be about 59 bp. Where 159 came from is unknown.\nAlso recorded: the paper names these yst-Pr2a / yst-Pr2b, not YE-1 / YE-2, and the target is the ystA enterotoxin reading frame. YE-1/YE-2 are the names first entered.</p></details>
