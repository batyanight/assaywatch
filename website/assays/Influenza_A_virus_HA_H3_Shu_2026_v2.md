## Mismatch distribution

Each oligo drawn base by base, 5′ to 3′. The shaded region is the one the scoring
penalizes more heavily, and it depends on the oligo. For a primer it is the 3′ window,
where a mismatch sits in the polymerase’s catalytic site. For a probe it is the central
third: a hydrolysis probe is never extended, its 3′ end carries the quencher and it is
cleaved by exonuclease activity, so what matters is duplex stability. Bar height is the
share of records that differ at that position. Bar color is what that costs, measured
as the fall in detection between records that differ there and records that do not:

<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-strong"></span> detection falls by 25 points or more</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-mid"></span> falls 10 to 25</span>
<span class="aw-key"><span class="aw-key-swatch aw-bar-weak"></span> little or no effect: variation the assay tolerates</span>

Hover for counts. A tall gray bar is a polymorphism the assay tolerates; a short red
bar is a position that matters.

## What was deposited, by lineage

Lineages here are sequence groups, not species or taxids. Each is a group of records
that look alike over the amplicon and its flanks with the oligo sites masked, cut at
0.05 Mash distance. Records can link one species to the next, so a group
can hold several; a percent in its name is the share of its top species. The groups
come from sequence this assay does not measure, so the mix below can move without the
assay changing. The dashed line on the chart above recomputes each year on the whole
period's mix: where the two lines part, what got sequenced changed, not what the assay
detects.

The defect is subtler and worth naming -- that message reads as a fact about the TAXON ("nothing recent was deposited") when it was a fact about OUR QUERY ("we cannot see what was deposited"), so a live assay sat unjudgeable and the report looked correct while saying so. Every query-side fix was measured and none survived. `H3N2[Title]` and `H3N2[Organism]` return 0. A bare `H3N2` term returns 33,737 for 2024-2026 but only 97.4% are really H3N2. Bare `H3`, `H5` and `H7` return 23,530, 1,862 and 778 for ALL dates against 71,868, 31,177 and 3,091 matching headers in one archive alone, so Entrez's handling of these strings cannot carry a denominator. The subtype is reliably stated in exactly one place, the record's own description. So the query is now the BROAD influenza A hemagglutinin query, shared with the other H-subtype entries (one archive, group q1f149635cb, 280,881 records, fetched once), and `target.title_requires: ['H3']` restricts this assay's population to its own subtype at screen time. The term is matched case-insensitively where not preceded by a letter or digit; a plain substring was measured WRONG, because strain identifiers carry the same letter-digit pairs as subtypes and `A/New Jersey/GKISBBBH74562/2026(H1N1)` contains "H7". Records whose description states no subtype are excluded rather than kept, since an untyped record cannot be known to be the target and a specific assay failing on unknown records reads as drift. The old archive q2fad34153e is orphaned by this change: nothing rescreens it and `scripts/storage_report.py` will list it. Full record and the before/after shares: docs/journal/2026-10-30-set3-scored.md and the subtype-filter journal.</p></details>
