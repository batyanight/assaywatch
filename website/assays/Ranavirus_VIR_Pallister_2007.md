*detected share 88.9% is below the 90% threshold, but its interval (80%-94%) still reaches it*

Trend across deposit years: by record falling (p = 0.192, 25 years); by footprint falling (p = 0.337). Reported for context; it is not an input to the verdict.

From the 2026-11-01 run. A status tells you where to look; it is not a result. [Known limitations](../limitations.md) reports how often these calls are correct.


## Scope

**Meant to detect:** Ranavirus variable intergenic region, for sequencing rather than detection.  
**Scored against:** every record retrieved.  
**Retrieved from:** Ranavirus (taxid 10492), every record.

This entry declares no scope restriction, so every record retrieved for its target taxid counts toward the denominator. Where an assay was designed for a narrower range than its taxid covers, that presents as low coverage rather than drift; see [scope and retrieval](../scoping.md).

Disclosures on this entry: `amplicon_multimodal`, `oligos_from_secondary_source`.

## Records reaching the statistics

1,467 records were screened for this assay and **250 (17%)** were evaluable, meaning both primers landed in the right orientation and size range. 1,217 produced no amplicon at all.

This matters because a record with one diverged primer is scored as a failure while a record with both diverged is excluded from the denominator entirely. The resulting bias is known, measured, and may not be correctable; see [known limitations](../limitations.md).

## What was deposited, by lineage

Lineages here are sequence groups, not species or taxids. Each is a group of records
that look alike over the amplicon and its flanks with the oligo sites masked, cut at
0.05 Mash distance. Records can link one species to the next, so a group
can hold several; a percent in its name is the share of its top species. The groups
come from sequence this assay does not measure, so the mix below can move without the
assay changing. The dashed line on the chart above recomputes each year on the whole
period's mix: where the two lines part, what got sequenced changed, not what the assay
detects.

**The largest group here, Ranavirus rana1 30%, holds several species names,** which may be
different species or one species under more than one name. The dashed line can't separate
a shift between species within a group. The table below shows the names in each group.

PROMOTION TO ACTIVE ATTEMPTED AND WITHDRAWN 2026-10-21. 

<details class="aw-curation"><summary>The full note, as the curator wrote it · in the order the curator wrote it, oldest first</summary><p><b>2026-10-21</b> PROMOTION TO ACTIVE ATTEMPTED AND WITHDRAWN 2026-10-21. The drift evidence supports it - detected share 90.0% (77%-96%) on 40 evaluable records in the 24-month window, status stable - and the entry meets every other schema precondition (taxid 10492, DOI verified, design_year 2007). It is held at draft by the amplicon defect below, found while checking the entry for promotion, which the validator correctly refuses to let an `active` entry carry. AMPLICON: THE DECLARED SCALAR IS WRONG AND IS HIDING RECORDS. Pallister et al. 2007 answers this directly (papers/, p. 4): "The amplification product obtained using the 16R and 16F primers was substantially smaller for BIV than for EHNV, ECV and ESV - around 200 bp for BIV, approximately 700 bp for EHNV and slightly larger for ECV and ESV." So the product length is SPECIES-DEPENDENT by design - this is the variable intergenic region, and the variation is the locus - and 200 bp is the BIV figure only. Measured on the 2026-10-21 run: of 128 records yielding a product the sizes are 244 bp (44), 228 (39), 212 (37), 227 (6), 209 (1) and 196 (1), all BIV-scale, and separately 61 records carry BOTH primer sites with no facing pair inside the 150-250 bp window the scalar implies ("sites found but no facing pair within 150-250 bp; both primers match"). Those 61 are the EHNV/ECV/ESV end of the assay and are counted as no_amplicon purely because of the declared size - a 48% understatement of the evaluable denominator. RESOLVED 2026-10-21 as amplicon_bp_range [186, 749], RANGE-ONLY. The 10492 archives were rescreened with the size window opened: detect/reduced products jump from 128 to 324 records spanning 196-739 bp - the EHNV/ECV/ESV ~700 bp end Pallister describes, recovered. NO species_sizes, because length does not cleanly name a species here: within Ranavirus micropterus1 alone the product ladders 212-308 bp, so the intergenic length varies by isolate, not by species as Pallister's four reference isotypes suggested. The range is the measured envelope + a 10 bp cushion (config.yaml amplicon: the measured size wins).</p><p><b>2026-10-21</b> PROMOTION STILL WAITS ON THE FULL RESCREEN, now for a different reason: the old 90.0%% stable counted only the BIV-scale products that detect well; including the ~700 bp end, much of which scores reduced or fail, will LOWER the real detected share, so the status has to be re-read on the next run before this is promoted. A related case, though not the identical defect: Alphavirus_nsP4_Sanchez-Seco_2001_round2 had a wrong SCALAR hiding 31 records on 2026-10-21 and was corrected to a scalar, not a range.</p><p><b>2026-10-21</b> OLIGO ATTRIBUTION, established 2026-10-21: Pallister credits 16R and 16F to Jancovich et al. 2005 in five places - twice in Methods ("the PCR primers used to amplify the variable region - 16R and 16F (Jancovich et al. 2005)") and in the legends of Figures 2, 3 and 4 - so this entry cites the paper the sequences were READ FROM, not one shown to have designed them, and oligos_designed_here is false. The credited paper, exactly as Pallister's reference list prints it and with no DOI printed there: "Jancovich J.K., Davidson E.W., Parameswaran N., Mao J., Chinchar G., Collins J.P., Jacobs B.L. &amp; Storfer A. (2005) Evidence for emergence of an amphibian iridoviral disease because of human-enhanced spread. Molecular Ecology 14, 213-224." THE CITATION IS DELIBERATELY NOT MOVED. Nothing here establishes that Jancovich 2005 prints these sequences: it is not in papers/, no DOI for it is verified, the two primer sequences in this entry were read from Pallister's own Methods text, and Pallister's Introduction describes Jancovich 2005 as having USED this variable region "to assign four different isotypes to salamander viruses" rather than as having designed primers for it. 16F/16R may trace back further still. Re-cite only once the Jancovich paper is on disk and its DOI is Crossref-verified, following Vairimorpha_bombi_18S_Klee_2006, which moved to Klee 2006 on exactly that evidence and still left oligos_designed_here null because Klee was unread.</p></details>
