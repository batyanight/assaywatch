# M1 shipped: the G:T/T:G wobble discounted to 0 (2026-10-21)

`scoring.mispair_penalty` ships non-empty for the first time:
`{"G:T": 0.0, "T:G": 0.0}`. Decision by Batya 2026-10-21: ship the version that
makes the most biological sense, and run the tier-2 re-screen after shipping
(not before). This records both.

## Why this table, and only this table

The 3'-terminal mismatch effect (Kwok 1990, Stadhouders 2010) is about whether
the polymerase extends from the 3' end, not about duplex melting. Among the 12
mispairs, the **G·T wobble** (primer G:template T, and primer T:template G) is
the one that is a genuine, near-Watson-Crick base pair: it stacks, and Taq
routinely extends from it. At the 3' terminus it behaves like a match, not a
block. That gives it a mechanism as well as the numbers, which is why it is the
one shipped first and alone:

- **FAP panel**: 12 of 14 false `fail`s rest only on T:G (`docs/2026-10-19-fap-bench-panel.md`).
- **Barrett panel**: 3 of 3 false `fail`s rescued (`docs/2026-10-20-scope-curation.md`).
- **Rahn 41 bench negatives**: zero specificity cost — no negative moves toward a
  false detect (`docs/2026-10-20-rahn-invA-bench-panel.md`).

What is **not** shipped, and why:

- **T:C, T:T** — Kwok calls them near-neutral, but they are pyrimidine-pyrimidine
  mismatches, not wobbles. No local bench panel exercises them and their
  specificity cost here is unmeasured, so they stay at 1.0. "Near-neutral in one
  paper's yield data" is weaker than "a wobble base pair with a mechanism."
- **A:G, G:A, C:C, A:A** — the severe suppressors. These are penalty *increases*,
  not discounts, and each needs its own bench panel before it ships.

M2 — reading this discount off a nearest-neighbour ΔG "for free" instead of
tabulating it — was killed at Step 0 (`docs/2026-10-21-M2-step0-killtest.md`):
NN melting inverts Kwok's order (A:A and A:G score as *stabilising*). So an
empirical per-mispair table is the instrument, and M1 is it.

A multiplier of 0 also exempts a 3'-terminal G:T/T:G from `terminal_mismatch_fails`,
by the rule in `config.yaml` — a mispair declared free does not impede extension,
so it must not fail the oligo at the one position where it matters most.

## Unit tests under the shipped default

Shipping a non-empty default surfaced three test files that had only ever seen
the empty table; all three were corrected to keep meaning what they say, none by
rubber-stamping a new number:

- `tests/test_config_keys.py` recursed into `mispair_penalty` and treated its
  data keys (`G:T`, `T:G`) as config settings read nowhere. Now it does not
  recurse into free-form data tables (`mispair_penalty`, `by_chemistry`,
  `sublineage`).
- `tests/test_score.py` MANIFEST fixtures hand-encode the position/count/terminal
  rules; a planted mismatch at a primer G is an incidental G:T the discount now
  zeroes. Those identity-agnostic fixtures score under `CFG_NO_MISPAIR` (the
  discount removed); the discount has its own dedicated tests, and the tier-2
  re-screen is its end-to-end check.
- `tests/test_per_chemistry_scoring.py`'s `write_config` now also clears
  `mispair_penalty`: every test there is about per-chemistry thresholds and the
  indel/terminal rules, none about identity.

Full suite after the change: **1478 passed**, ruff clean, validator 208/208.
