# Held-out set 3 scored: the first drift case the tool has ever caught, and the second cannot be judged at all

2026-10-30, after the four influenza entries were signed off. They are promoted, the new H3
group is fetched, the four assays are screened, and set 3 is scored. One row agrees, one is
undecided, and the reason the second is undecided turns out to be a defect in three live
entries rather than anything about Shu 2026.

## The result

| row | as of | expect | status | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `INFLUENZA_A_M1_Ward_2004` (Yang 2014) | 2013-09-06 | fails | **`declining`** | **agrees** |
| `INFLUENZA_A_HA_H3_CDC_Shu_2026` (Shu 2026) | 2026-10-27 | fails | `no recent data` | undecided |

    declining calls that were documented failures (PPV): 1/1 = 100% (95% CrI 15%-100%)
    documented failures caught: 1/2 = 50% (95% CrI 6%-94%)
      among the cases it judged at all: 1/1 = 100% (95% CrI 15%-100%)
      caught by the drift flag itself (declining): 1/2
    failure cases by mechanism: drift 2
    working assays called declining (false alarms): 0/0

**This is the first time the tool has caught a documented failure by the drift flag itself.**
Every previous catch in the project was credited to `low coverage`, which is a statement about
how much of a family's deposited diversity an assay reaches, not about decay over time. The
premise AssayWatch is built on has a confirming case for the first time.

Two intervals are wide on one row each and should be read that way. 1/1 is not a precision
estimate.

## The internal control, which is what makes the Ward row worth trusting

As of 2013-09-06, over the 43,786 records deposited before then and the 5,847 in the recent
24-month window:

| assay | status | detected share | note |
|---|---|---|---|
| `INFLUENZA_A_M1_Ward_2004` | **`declining`** | 86.2% (85-87%) | upper bound below the 90% threshold; change from baseline -6% to -4% |
| `INFLUENZA_A_M1_Yang_2014` | **`stable`** | 91.6% (91-92%) | the documented redesign, same records |

The two entries differ in nothing but their oligos: same retrieval group, same archive, same
date window, same scoring config, same probe chemistry. So the verdict difference is
attributable to the primer and probe sequences and to nothing else. The assay Yang reported
losing sensitivity reads `declining`; the degenerate redesign Yang built to fix it reads
`stable`. That is the shape a true positive should have, and it is stronger evidence than the
verdict alone.

Registry-wide, over all 187,534 matrix records and all dates:

| | detect | reduced | fail | no_amplicon | indeterminate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ward 2004 | 110,359 | 16,651 | **59,507** | 993 | 24 |
| Yang 2014 | 176,650 | 8,391 | **1,486** | 994 | 13 |

The redesign turns 58,021 failures into detections. Read as corroboration of direction, not as
a verdict: it is every date pooled, which is not how a verdict is computed.

## Why the Shu row cannot be judged, and why that is not about Shu

`no recent data`: as of 2026-10-27 the recent window holds fewer records than
`drift.min_records_for_drift`. For seasonal A(H3N2) that is absurd on its face, so it was run
down.

**The H3 archive stops in 2018.** Deposit years in the group this entry fetched: 6,160 in
2017, 870 in 2018, **56 in 2019, 64 in 2020, 1 in 2021, none after**. Shu's drift runs
2020-2025, which is exactly the span the archive does not cover.

**The cause is the subtype-taxid query, and it is measured.** Both H3 entries restrict to the
seven H3Nx subtype taxids, which is how the Tsukamoto H5 and H7 entries are written and is why
it was done that way:

| query | records |
|---|---|
| seven H3Nx subtype taxids AND HA, 2024-2026 | **0** |
| `txid11320` AND HA, 2024-2026 | **95,155** |
| seven H3Nx subtype taxids AND HA, all dates | 44,633 |

NCBI has stopped assigning subtype taxids to new influenza deposits; recent records sit under
the parent `txid11320`. The caveat both H3 entries already carried — "a subtype-taxid query
misses records whose names give no N type" — is not a footnote. It costs everything after 2021.

## The same defect is live on two existing entries, and that is the finding worth keeping

Newest deposit in each influenza retrieval group on disk:

| group | query shape | records | newest deposit |
|---|---|---|---|
| `q6552a5c4af` — H5 Tsukamoto 2010 | subtype taxids | 8,490 | **2021** |
| `q3b4e39b708` — H7 Tsukamoto 2010 | subtype taxids | 3,425 | **2020** |
| `qdf5abd73c1` — NP Tsukamoto 2010 | title / gene | 194,149 | 2026 |
| `q2ebd5918b3` — matrix (Spackman, Ward, Yang) | title / gene | 187,534 | 2026 |

Every influenza entry whose query names subtype taxids has an archive that ended four to six
years ago; every one that queries by title or gene is current. **So the H5 and H7 assays'
verdicts in every report this project has produced read `no recent data`.** Both committed
example runs name the Oct 2021 cutoff in the note beside each assay, so **no wrong verdict was
ever published** -- a claim an earlier draft of this journal made and which is corrected here.
What the report could not say is that the cutoff was a property of OUR RETRIEVAL QUERY and not
of the taxon, so four live assays sat unjudgeable while the output read as correct. Neither can
show recent drift by construction. For H5 in particular that is a material thing not to have
known.

**Not fixed here, because the fix is a real design decision and the obvious version of it is
wrong.** Broadening to `txid11320 AND HA` makes the denominator every influenza A
hemagglutinin record, so a correctly specific H3 subtyping assay would read as detecting a
small share of its own archive and look like it was failing. That is the
`scope_narrower_than_taxid` problem the registry already has a flag for, and it is the reason
the subtype restriction existed. What an H3 assay needs is its records restricted to H3
viruses by something other than a taxid NCBI no longer assigns — the strain name in the record
title, or the scope layer. On the roadmap, with this measurement attached.

## Neither scope could be honoured, for a third reason

Both rows carry a `scope_taxid` and neither was used:

    INFLUENZA_A_M1_Ward_2004 @ 2013-09-06: no per-lineage statistics for taxid 114727 among 1 lineages
    INFLUENZA_A_HA_H3_CDC_Shu_2026 @ 2026-10-27: no per-lineage statistics for this assay

For the matrix group the lineage layer sees **one** lineage. Two things cause it: **18 of the
19 metadata files in that archive predate the `taxid` column** (the schema gained `taxid` and
`is_patent` later, and the old files were never refetched), and the one file that has it
reports all 4,009 of its records as `11320`, the parent. The H3 archive, fetched today under
current code, does carry strain-level taxids.

So **set 3 did not test the scoped matrix after all**, which is what it was opened to do. That
claim has been corrected in the file rather than left standing. A scoped score on an influenza
assay needs the lineage layer to resolve influenza records below the species, and for this
archive it cannot.

## What was run, for reproducibility

Not a pipeline run: a targeted screen of the four new assays only, so the Sunday job remains
the confirming rescreen for everything.

- `assaywatch curate` on each of the four, Crossref verifying all four DOIs.
- `git mv pending/ -> registry/`; validator **218 live, 0 problems** and **10 pending, 0
  problems**.
- `assaywatch retrieve` for the two H3 entries: **44,627 of 44,627 available**, new group
  `q2fad34153e`.
- `assaywatch screen --only` for Ward and Yang over the 19 matrix archives (375,087 rows) and
  for the two H3 entries over the new archive.
- `collect.concatenate_screens` over the 20 screens into one calls table, then
  `scripts/retrospective.py --cases docs/validation-cases-heldout-3.tsv --calls <that>`.

The calls table is scratch and is not committed; its numbers are above.
