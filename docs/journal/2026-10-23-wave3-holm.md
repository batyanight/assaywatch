# Wave 3: Holm replaces Benjamini-Hochberg, and changes nothing yet (2026-10-23)

Plan items 3.1 and 3.2.

**The rule.** `lineage.worst_lineage_verdict` fails an assay when ANY of its lineages is
significantly below the threshold. It corrected the per-lineage binomial p-values with
Benjamini-Hochberg at statsmodels' default 0.05, both hardcoded. BH controls the
expected **share** of false rejections among rejections; the event that decides the
status is **at least one** false rejection, which needs family-wise control. Holm (1979)
gives it at no cost in power relative to Bonferroni.

**The change.** `lineage.multiple_testing: holm` and `lineage.alpha: 0.05` in
`config.yaml`, read through `LineageOptions` and onto each `AssayDrift`, and passed to
`worst_lineage_verdict` through one helper, `AssayDrift._worst_lineage`. The method and
level no longer live in code. A test builds five lineages at p = 0.021-0.042 against
0.90: BH fails the assay (its largest p is under 0.05), Holm does not (its smallest p
must clear 0.05/5 = 0.01).

**The measurement, on the frozen table.** Tier-2 identical row for row. Report statuses
identical assay by assay, overrides included. The 20 lineages that drive overrides are
mostly far below the threshold; the largest p among them is 0.014, and Holm still
rejects it. The wrong family made no difference to today's output, which is why it went
unnoticed — and why fixing it now, before the sub-lineage resolvers multiply the number
of lineages each assay tests (3.5, 3.6), is the point.

**What it does not do.** Nothing controls error across the 200-odd assays; the report
header says so (wave 1). Holm is a stopgap: partial pooling
(`docs/bayesian-scoring-plan.md` C1) is the intended replacement, which is why no
non-inferiority margin was tuned here (plan item 3.3, skipped).
