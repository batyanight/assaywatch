# Assays offered by veterinary and wildlife labs: leads not in the registry (2026-10-06)

Source of every row: web-search snippets and PubMed lookups made by search agents, one per group of
institutions, followed by one verifier agent per distinct lead. Companion file:
`docs/2026-10-06-lab-leads.tsv` (188 rows).

**Nothing here has been entered in `registry/`.** The usual rule applies: read the sequences from the
paper's own table, measure `amplicon_bp` on real records, verify the DOI against Crossref, and only then
write a reference.

## How far to trust it

- **Test menus were not read.** `WebFetch` is blocked for the lab hosts (the egress proxy), so the agents
  saw only search-result text. No lab's complete molecular menu was read. A pathogen missing from this
  list is not evidence that a lab does not offer it. Each agent's own account of what it could not see is
  in the `limits` field of the workflow result (kept in the session transcript, not committed).
- **The safety review of the subagents' work could not be run** (the reviewer refused or timed out for
  nearly all of them). I scanned the returned text for instruction-like content and found none, and
  checked the citations below. That is not the same as a review.
- **Citations.** Of 121 distinct DOIs the verifiers gave for kept leads, 45 were confirmed against PubMed
  by me (the PMID the agent gave matches the PMID PubMed returns for the DOI; 0 mismatches). The other 76
  could not be checked that way, because the PubMed ID converter resolves only PMC-indexed papers, and
  are marked `agent-reported, not independently confirmed` in the TSV. 49 kept leads cite no paper at
  all. Do not enter a reference from this file without running `scripts/resolve_citations.py`.
- **"In registry" is a grep, by the agents.** 5 of the 5 high-priority pathogens I re-checked myself
  (`grep -ril` over `registry/` and the List 2 worklist): zero hits for each. The `partial` calls (pathogen
  present, different target) were not re-checked.
- **Lab attribution is often wrong.** For at least three of the five high-priority leads (Campylobacter, Borrelia, Bartonella) the
  verifier found that the search agent named the wrong lab (see below). The TSV column is `lab_named_by_search` for that
  reason.
- Verifiers ran at low effort. "primer_sequences_in_paper" is `unknown` for most medium rows; only one
  medium row (Anaplasma marginale) has it as `yes`.

## The five high-priority leads

These have a paper whose existence I confirmed in PubMed, open access in PMC, with primers reported as
printed in the text. I have not read the primer tables myself; the sequences quoted by the verifiers are
in the TSV `reason` column and must be re-read from the paper.

1. **Babesia odocoilei, 18S rRNA, conventional PCR, 306 bp.** Burgess et al., J Vet Diagn Invest
   2021;33(6):1188-92, PMID 34550025,
   [10.1177/10406387211032927](https://doi.org/10.1177/10406387211032927). Canadian Wildlife Health
   Cooperative / Western College of Veterinary Medicine (Bollinger). Works on formalin-fixed tissue. The
   abstract warns of nonspecific banding against other Babesia and Theileria, so specificity needs care.
2. **Borrelia spp., flaB nested PCR.** Cleveland et al., Vet Sci 2020;7(3):82, PMID 32629926,
   [10.3390/vetsci7030082](https://doi.org/10.3390/vetsci7030082). Run at the Southeastern Cooperative
   Wildlife Disease Study (University of Georgia), not at Penn Vet as the search agent said. Conventional
   nested PCR, no probe; the method cites earlier papers whose primers should be traced.
3. **Dichelobacter nodosus (ovine footrot), 16S rRNA TaqMan.** Frosth et al., Acta Vet Scand 2012;54:6,
   PMID 22293440, [10.1186/1751-0147-54-6](https://doi.org/10.1186/1751-0147-54-6). SVA Sweden with
   Norwegian and Danish partners. A sheep pathogen: check scope.
4. **Bartonella spp., gltA SYBR qPCR, about 380 bp.** Jeeves et al., BMC Res Notes 2024;17:184, PMID
   38956715, [10.1186/s13104-024-06841-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13104-024-06841-5). The paper's main
   contribution is a blood-clot DNA extraction; the primers come from an earlier paper the verifier could
   not identify. Trace that original source before citing.
5. **Campylobacter jejuni, canine feces TaqMan rtPCR.** Lawhon et al., J Vet Diagn Invest 2026;38(5):
   891-899, PMID 41983274,
   [10.1177/10406387261434179](https://doi.org/10.1177/10406387261434179). Method lab is Texas A&M (with
   Cornell, Ohio ADDL, NJ AHDL and others as participants), not UConn as the search agent said. The paper
   says its target gene was "identified in previous studies" and the gene name did not come through, so
   the original primer paper is still to be found. Dogs, not wildlife.

## Cornell

Cornell's own Wildlife Health Laboratory lists PCRs offered (avian influenza, bluetongue, Bordetella,
Leptospira, malignant catarrhal fever, parapox, *Ophidiomyces*, *Salmonella* and others) and, per one
search summary that I could not re-read on the page, 15 under development: pigeon circovirus,
lymphoproliferative disease virus, avian poxvirus, reticuloendotheliosis virus, crow reovirus, duck
enteritis virus, herpesvirus, poxvirus, parapoxvirus, skunk adenovirus, mammalian orthoreovirus,
ranavirus (FV3), python nidovirus, white-nose syndrome fungal PCR and *Babesia microti*.

No target gene or primer reference was visible for any Cornell menu test. These are the most likely
place where a lab runs something the registry lacks, but the evidence is that a test exists, not what
primers it uses. The practical route is to email cwhl@cornell.edu and ask for the method citation, or
for the protocol if it is unpublished.

## Gaps in coverage

- The Cornell AHDC full menu, the UC Davis PCR laboratory's menu (over 100 tests), WADDL, OVDL and CSU
  menus were never read in full.
- Several PubMed affiliation searches hit a rate limit and were not retried, so published Cornell,
  Oregon State and Colorado State assay papers are under-searched.
- No wildlife- or zoo-specific PCR menu was found at the seven Midwest labs searched.
